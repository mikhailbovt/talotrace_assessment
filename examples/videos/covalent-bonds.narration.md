# Why do atoms form covalent bonds?

Complete Kokoro af_heart narration. Times are measured from the final video.

## 1. Sharing, Not Transferring

0.00–14.55 seconds

Atoms form covalent bonds when two atoms have similar tendencies to attract electrons. Rather than transferring electrons, the atoms share them between both nuclei. That shared electron pair is the covalent bond.

## 2. The H₂ Example

15.62–30.62 seconds

Hydrogen gives a simple example. Each H atom contributes one electron. The two electrons are shared equally in H₂, so each electron is attracted to both positive nuclei. This is a pure covalent bond.

## 3. Attraction and Repulsion

31.67–46.22 seconds

As the atoms approach, their 1s orbitals overlap and attraction lowers the system’s potential energy. But if the nuclei come too close, electron–electron and nucleus–nucleus repulsions raise the energy.

## 4. Orbital Overlap Makes the Link

47.29–60.87 seconds

A covalent bond forms when an orbital on each atom overlaps and their single electrons form a shared pair. That negatively charged pair is attracted to both positive nuclei, physically linking the atoms.

## 5. Why the Bond Is Stable

61.92–77.49 seconds

At this arrangement, pulling the atoms apart requires added energy to overcome their attraction. So atoms form the covalent bond because sharing electron density with both nuclei creates a lower-energy, stable H₂ arrangement.
