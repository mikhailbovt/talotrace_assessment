# How does the pH scale work?

Complete Kokoro af_heart narration. Times are measured from the final video.

## 1. What pH Measures

0.00–20.90 seconds

pH tells us about the molar concentration of hydronium ions in an aqueous solution. The definition is pH equals negative log, base ten, of hydronium concentration. The brackets mean concentration. So pH is not a direct concentration number; it is a compact logarithmic way to report it.

## 2. Why the Scale Is Logarithmic

21.96–44.86 seconds

Because the scale is logarithmic, equal steps in pH represent equal powers of ten in hydronium concentration. A lower pH means a greater hydronium concentration. A solution is acidic when hydronium concentration is greater than hydroxide concentration, and basic when it is lower. So pH is not a linear ruler.

## 3. Neutral at 25 °C

45.92–68.09 seconds

Neutrality means equal hydronium and hydroxide concentrations. At twenty-five degrees Celsius, pure water has pH seven and is neutral. This balanced diagram shows equal concentrations of the two ions. The temperature qualification matters: pH seven here is specifically the neutral reference at twenty-five degrees Celsius.

## 4. Reading One pH Step

69.17–90.17 seconds

Compare these two hydronium concentrations. At pH three, the concentration is ten to the negative three molar; at pH four, it is ten to the negative four molar. Increasing pH by one therefore means a tenfold decrease in hydronium concentration. Equal pH steps do not form a linear concentration scale.

## 5. How pH Is Displayed and Measured

91.21–114.53 seconds

Finally, pH can be measured directly with a pH meter, or visually estimated with colored indicators and pH paper. Zero to fourteen is a common display range, not a universal limit: values such as pH minus one and pH fifteen can also occur. The core rule remains unchanged: lower pH means greater hydronium concentration.
