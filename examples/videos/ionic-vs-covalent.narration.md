# What is the difference between ionic and covalent bonding?

Complete Kokoro af_heart narration. Times are measured from the final video.

## 1. The Core Difference

0.00–22.00 seconds

Ionic and covalent bonds differ mainly in how electrons behave. In ionic bonding, electrons are transferred between atoms, producing charged ions whose opposite charges attract. In covalent bonding, atoms share electrons; the shared electrons are attracted by both nuclei. The result is a different kind of particle arrangement.

## 2. Ionic Bonding: A Lattice of Ions

23.04–45.32 seconds

In an ionic compound, a positive cation and a negative anion are held together by electrostatic attraction. Ionic solids form crystalline lattices, so sodium chloride is pictured as an extended alternating array of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions—not as separate NaCl molecules. In the solid, the ions are not free to move easily.

## 3. Covalent Bonding: Sharing in H₂

46.38–69.12 seconds

In a covalent bond, atoms share electrons. H₂ is a clear example: each hydrogen contributes one electron, and the two electrons occupy space around both nuclei. As the atoms approach, attraction lowers the potential energy; if they come too close, repulsion raises it. The stable bond distance is where the potential energy is lowest.

## 4. Electronegativity Creates a Continuum

70.17–96.54 seconds

Electronegativity—the tendency to attract electrons in a bond—helps place bonds on a continuum. A very small difference gives pure, nonpolar covalent sharing. An intermediate difference gives polar covalent sharing, with unequal sharing. A large difference is characterized as ionic. Rough guideposts are below 0.4, 0.4 to 1.8, and above 1.8, but the source notes exceptions.

## 5. Some Substances Contain Both

97.58–125.18 seconds

Here is the compact comparison: ionic bonding is electrostatic attraction among oppositely charged ions in a lattice; covalent bonding is shared electron density between atoms. One substance can contain both. In potassium nitrate, K⁺ and NO₃⁻ are held together ionically, while the nitrogen–oxygen bonds inside nitrate are covalent. So ask two questions: are there ions in a lattice, and are electrons shared between atoms?
